# ProQOL, Russian adaptation (2023)

- **Decision:** prepared. The adaptation article contains the complete Russian text and an explicit scoring key.
- **Russian text:** [“Russian adaptation of the ProQOL (Professional Quality of Life) scale by B. Stamm” — questionnaire appendix, pp. 197–198](https://psyjournals.ru/journals/vspu_psychology/archive/2023_n2/vspu_psychology_2023_n2_Pankratova_Nikolayeva.pdf), A. A. Pankratova and M. E. Nikolaeva, *Vestnik of Saint Petersburg University. Psychology*, 2023, vol. 13, no. 2, pp. 183–198, DOI [10.21638/spbu16.2023.204](https://doi.org/10.21638/spbu16.2023.204). Text cross-checked against the [ProQOL questionnaire form](https://psytests.org/work/proqol-bl.html); the form is used only for wording verification, while the adaptation article supplies the key.
- **Original instrument:** [The Concise ProQOL Manual, 2nd edition](https://www.cacnd.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/ProQOL5-Manual.pdf), Beth Hudnall Stamm, 2010 (original English ProQOL v.5). This registration implements the published Russian adaptation, whose key differs from the original.
- **Items and responses:** 30 statements about frequency during the previous 30 days; five choices from “Never” (1) to “Very often” (5).
- **Scales and formula:** the four primary scales are unweighted sums with no reverse-coded items: job satisfaction — 1, 3, 4, 6, 16, 20, 22, 24, 27, 29; profession satisfaction — 12, 15, 17, 18, 30; secondary trauma — 10, 11, 19, 21, 25, 26, 28; burnout — 2, 7, 8, 9, 13, 14. Second-order sums: professional satisfaction = job satisfaction + profession satisfaction; compassion fatigue = secondary trauma + burnout. The adaptation authors state that all Russian items are direct and that the scale membership differs from the original key.
- **Scope:** validated primarily with counseling psychologists. For other helping professions, the article recommends adapting wording to the profession and rechecking factor structure. No diagnostic cutoffs or norms were added.
- **Manual check:** answers cycling 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 across items 1–30 produce primary totals 30, 15, 23, and 19; second-order totals are 45 and 42.
